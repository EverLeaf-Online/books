# Book 24 — River & Wetland Animals — Art QA Scorecard

Batches: B047–B048
Illustrations: 60
Status: Not Reviewed

## Page-level checks

Every illustration must pass all checks before approval:

- Correct subject and recognizable species/type.
- Child-friendly anatomy appropriate for ages 4–8.
- Bold, smooth black outlines with thinner interior detail lines.
- Pure black line art on white; no grayscale, shading, hatching, gradients, textures, words, signatures, or watermarks.
- Main subject occupies roughly 55–70% of the page.
- Large, simple, enclosed coloring spaces; no clutter or excessive tiny details.
- All anatomy stays inside the 0.50-inch safe area; no accidental cropping.
- Lower caption area stays clear for separately typeset animal name and fact.
- Water, reeds, logs, rocks, lily pads, and shoreline elements remain simple and sparse.

## Subject-specific checks

- Semi-aquatic mammals: feet, tails, whiskers, ears, and swimming posture must fit the named animal.
- Crocodilians/turtles/snakes: snout, shell, limbs, scale pattern, and body shape must be plausible.
- Amphibians: toe structure, gills where appropriate, and body proportions must match the named subject.
- Wetland birds: bill shape, legs, feet, wings, and wading/perching posture must fit the listed bird.
- Fish/invertebrates: fins, barbels, claws, shell, and body segmentation must be recognizable and simplified cleanly.
- `Cottonmouth` is the normalized name for the former `Water Moccasin` entry; use a heavy-bodied cottonmouth in a neutral non-striking pose.
- Species-normalized entries must visually match their exact labels: North American River Otter, Spiny Softshell Turtle, Northern Map Turtle, Northern Leopard Frog, Green Tree Frog, Eastern Newt, and Amazon Kingfisher.
