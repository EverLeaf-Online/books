# Book 25 — Mountain Animals — Art QA Scorecard

## Scope

- Book: **25 — Mountain Animals**
- Batches: **B049 (subjects 1–30)** and **B050 (subjects 31–60)**
- Target: ages 4–8, 8.5×11 portrait, black-and-white line art
- Final gate: **30/30 approved in each batch** before interior assembly is considered art-complete

## Per-Image QA

Each image must pass all of the following:

- [ ] Correct subject identity and recognizable species/breed traits
- [ ] Anatomically plausible body proportions and limb count
- [ ] No duplicated, fused, missing, or malformed limbs/features
- [ ] Bold, smooth outer contours with thinner simple interior lines
- [ ] Large enclosed coloring areas appropriate for ages 4–8
- [ ] Main subject occupies roughly 55–70% of usable drawing area
- [ ] Important anatomy remains inside the 0.50-inch safe-content zone
- [ ] Lower page remains clear enough for separately typeset name/fact
- [ ] Background contains only simple, large mountain habitat elements
- [ ] No tiny clutter or excessive detail
- [ ] Pure black linework on white background
- [ ] No grayscale, shading, hatching, gradients, or texture fills
- [ ] No text, letters, numbers, captions, logos, signatures, or watermarks
- [ ] No decorative border
- [ ] No important anatomy cropped by the page edge
- [ ] Filename exactly matches `artwork_manifest.csv`
- [ ] Output master is suitable for 2550×3300 PNG production

## Mountain-Specific Accuracy Checks

- [ ] Hoofed mountain mammals show plausible hooves/horns for the named species
- [ ] Cats, canids, and bears have species-appropriate ears, tails, paws, and coat outlines
- [ ] Mountain birds show plausible bill, wing, foot, crest, and tail structures
- [ ] Raptors/vultures are not drawn with generic songbird anatomy
- [ ] Ptarmigan/snowcock/pheasant-type birds remain visually distinct
- [ ] Reptiles and amphibians have correct limb/body plans and are not mixed together
- [ ] Alpine habitat cues are simple and plausible: rocks, slopes, snow patches, conifers, meadows, streams
- [ ] No habitat element obscures the primary subject
